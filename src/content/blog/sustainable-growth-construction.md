Sustainable growth will not come from undercutting on price — it will come from converting a closed, dealer-controlled trade into an open, demand-driven ecosystem.

This perspective draws on over 30 years of hands-on experience across India's housing, building, and construction landscape — spanning new product and technology introductions and sector-specific market development. That deep, on-ground association with the ecosystem informs the view that India's building materials industry is in the middle of a structural shift — moving away from fragmented, unorganised trade toward a more consolidated, brand-conscious market. The opportunity across categories such as panels, surfaces, tiles, and allied materials is significant, but building a position that lasts — not just one that enters — remains the harder task.

The biggest hurdle to durable growth is the entrenched dealer network, which functions as a gatekeeper, safeguarding old trade equations and controlling the route to the end customer. Overcoming this calls for a fundamental change in approach: moving from a traditional "push" model, where companies chase dealers for shelf space, to a "pull" model, where market forces and end-user preference do the heavy lifting. Sustained over time, this pull-based approach rests on three interconnected pillars.

## Pillar 1: Winning Over the Specifiers

Established players often rely on decades-old dealer ties that are tough to break through conventional selling. A more durable route is to build influence with the people who actually decide what gets used on a project — architects, interior designers, structural engineers, and contractors. Once a specifier backs a brand, dealers have little choice but to stock it, putting demand creation ahead of distribution. Sustaining this advantage requires a specialised team — one that pairs technical credibility with the persistence to engage these stakeholders consistently, using tools like product training, specification assistance, and digital engagement to keep the brand top of mind over the long run.

## Pillar 2: Stepping Away from Price-Led Competition

Chasing volume through price cuts is a race few can win sustainably — it almost always favours the largest, most cost-efficient producer, and it erodes margins for everyone else. The more enduring position is to compete on what the product actually delivers: resistance to moisture, durability, finish quality, or fit-for-purpose performance. This calls for genuine product depth within the team and the skill to frame these advantages as long-term value rather than short-term cost, shifting the customer's decision criteria away from price alone and toward lasting reliability.

## Pillar 3: Building Direct Channels to the Market

Digital platforms and e-commerce are steadily reshaping how building materials reach buyers, opening a direct route to workshops, OEMs, and even individual consumers — bypassing layers of traditional distribution. Built well, this channel adds resilience to the business model rather than just reach. It demands its own set of capabilities: managing online presence, pricing strategy, and customer experience, while keeping a tight grip on brand consistency and after-sales reliability in a space where trust has to be earned and maintained without face-to-face interaction.

## Conclusion

Sustainable growth in India's construction materials space comes from creating demand at the source — with the specifiers who influence buying decisions — competing on genuine value rather than price, and building direct digital pathways alongside the traditional trade. Together, these shifts turn a dealer-controlled market into one shaped, over the long term, by brand pull and customer preference.
